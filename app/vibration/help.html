Recommended workflow

  1. Start periodic capture in Spectrum (FFT) → Periodic capture (e.g. every 60–300 s, Average 2–4). Each capture series is one point in Grafana.
  2. Record a baseline with the machine in good condition at its usual speed (at least 20–30 minutes). Note velocity RMS, the strongest peak frequencies and the band distribution per axis.
  3. Watch the trends, not single values. A change against this machine's own baseline matters more than any absolute number.
  4. Compare peak frequencies in Hz between operating conditions and across axes.
Rule of thumb used in condition monitoring: a value that doubles against the baseline deserves attention, a value that rises four times needs action, whatever its absolute level.

Live values (sensor registers)

Read from the sensor at the poll interval (Bus form). The sensor computes them itself, specified for 5–100 Hz.

ValueUnitMeaning
Vibration velocitymm/sOverall vibration velocity per axis. The best single indicator of mechanical condition of rotating machines.
Vibration displacementµmMovement amplitude. Large at low frequencies; useful when investigating low-frequency motion and looseness.
Dominant frequencyHzStrongest frequency the sensor found. Compare it with the peak frequencies from the raw FFT.
AccelerationgHeavily smoothed; mainly shows mounting orientation (≈1 g on the vertical axis). Not a vibration measure.
Temperature°CChip temperature of the sensor, roughly the housing temperature. A slow rise can follow bearing problems.

On a sensor at rest the statistics are noisy (for example kurtosis on the gravity axis). Judge them only with the machine running.

Spectrum (FFT)

The collector reads 1024 raw acceleration samples per axis from the sensor (one capture, about 4.2 s) and computes the spectrum itself.

ItemMeaning
Sample rateMeasured for every capture (≈ 246 Hz). "(default)" means it could not be measured and 246 Hz was used.
Length / resolution4.16 s of signal, 0.24 Hz between spectrum lines. Peaks closer than about 0.5 Hz merge.
Frequency range0 to ≈ 123 Hz (half the sample rate). Peaks are reported from 2 Hz.
Averaged N capturesN captures in a row, averaged. Steady lines keep their height, noise and random spikes shrink (about √N). Resolution does not improve.
g / mm/sAcceleration amplitude or velocity amplitude. Velocity emphasises low frequencies, acceleration high ones (bearings, impacts).
Top peaksThree strongest lines per axis with frequency and amplitude.
Velocity RMSRMS of the velocity spectrum from the configured lower limit (2 Hz by default) to the Nyquist frequency.
Band RMSAcceleration RMS in 2–10, 10–25, 25–50, 50–100 and 100–123 Hz. Shows where the energy is and where it moves.

Reading the chart

  • Compare keeps the scale across captures and expands it for larger signals. Use Reset scale to start over or Auto to fit the current capture.
  • The rise at 0–1 Hz is window leakage of the removed gravity; ignore it.
  • Compare axes: the radial axes (perpendicular to the shaft) show unbalance; the axial axis shows misalignment more strongly.

Waveform (time signal)

Spectrum (FFT) → Waveform shows the last capture (4.16 s) as acceleration (g, gravity removed), velocity (mm/s) or displacement (µm) per axis. Velocity and displacement are integrated from the acceleration in the frequency domain above 2 Hz, because integration would otherwise turn slow drift into large false movement.

  • Smooth sine-like curve often corresponds to one dominant frequency.
  • Short sharp spikes, repeating: impacts from a bearing defect, a damaged gear tooth or rubbing. Best seen in acceleration.
  • Beating (amplitude rising and falling slowly): two close frequencies.
  • Clipped, flattened or asymmetric peaks: looseness or rub.
  • Different shape every capture: unstable process or looseness.

Orbits

Spectrum (FFT) → Orbit plots broadband displacement in each pair of axes (XY, XZ, YZ) for the last capture. The minor-to-major axis ratio ranges from 0 for a line to 1 for a circle.

Sensor harmonics (spectrum energy)

The sensor reports energy at eight focus points, six focus bands and a total. Use those measurements as trends. The raw FFT provides the peak frequencies and fixed band limits in Hz.

Limits of the sensor

  • The live registers are specified for 5–100 Hz. Use the raw FFT to inspect lower frequencies.
  • Spectrum up to ≈ 123 Hz: rolling-bearing defect frequencies and their resonances are often higher, so kurtosis and crest factor are the main bearing indicators.
  • One capture is 4.16 s and 0.24 Hz resolution; it cannot be made longer. Averaging reduces noise only.
  • During a capture (≈ 4.5 s) polling of all sensors on the bus pauses; within a series every sensor is polled once between captures.
  • Mount the sensor rigidly on the bearing housing (magnet on a flat, clean surface or screw). A loose mount looks like looseness.

Grafana dashboard

Vibration (WTVB01-485): select the sensor at the top (the only setting). The dominant frequency panel shows the sensor registers per axis. The section Spectrum (FFT) · raw FIFO captures shows peak frequencies and amplitudes, band RMS, velocity RMS and capture settings. Hover over a point in FFT peak frequencies to see the amplitude along with the frequency.

Simulation

For tests without a machine set VIBRATION_SIM_PORTS=1 in .env, rebuild the collector and select a sim:// port in the Bus form. sim://frequency?hz=5.25 generates a reference signal at 5.25 Hz. Adding &scenario=faults runs a 30 minute sequence: normal, unbalance, misalignment, frequency drop, looseness, bearing impacts, communication faults, normal. The current stage is shown in the Port tile. Rename the sensor for a session so simulated data stay apart from real data, and select the real port afterwards — the real sensor is not polled meanwhile.
