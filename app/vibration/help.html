Recommended workflow

  1. Nothing to enter. The roll speed (1x) is found from the vibration itself in every capture series, and the velocity RMS range follows from it (2 Hz up to 600 rpm, 10 Hz above, per ISO 10816-3). Line speed and roll diameter are not needed.
  2. Start periodic capture (e.g. every 60–300 s, Average 2–4). Each capture series is one point in Grafana.
  3. Record a baseline with the machine in good condition at its usual speed (at least 20–30 minutes). Note velocity RMS, 1x and 2x amplitudes and the band distribution per axis.
  4. Watch the trends, not single values. A change against this machine's own baseline matters more than any absolute number.
  5. Check speed steps. Lines that move with the dashed 1x/2x/3x markers come from the roll; lines that stay put come from elsewhere.
Rule of thumb used in condition monitoring: a value that doubles against the baseline deserves attention, a value that rises four times needs action, whatever its absolute level.

Live values (sensor registers)

Read from the sensor about once per second (poll interval). The sensor computes them itself, specified for 5–100 Hz.

ValueUnitMeaning
Vibration velocitymm/sOverall vibration velocity per axis. The best single indicator of mechanical condition for 10–1000 rpm machines.
Vibration displacementµmMovement amplitude. Large at low frequencies; useful for slow rolls and looseness.
Dominant frequencyHzStrongest frequency the sensor found. Compare with the roll 1x / 2x.
AccelerationgHeavily smoothed; mainly shows mounting orientation (≈1 g on the vertical axis). Not a vibration measure.
Temperature°CChip temperature of the sensor, roughly the housing temperature. A slow rise can follow bearing problems.

On a sensor at rest the statistics are noisy (for example kurtosis on the gravity axis). Judge them only with the machine running.

Spectrum (FFT)

The collector reads 1024 raw acceleration samples per axis from the sensor (one capture, about 4.2 s) and computes the spectrum itself.

ItemMeaning
Sample rateMeasured for every capture (≈ 246 Hz). "(default)" means it could not be measured and 246 Hz was used.
Length / resolution4.16 s of signal, 0.24 Hz between spectrum lines. Peaks closer than about 0.5 Hz merge.
Frequency range0 to ≈ 123 Hz (half the sample rate). Peaks are reported from 2 Hz.
Averaged N capturesN captures in a row, averaged. Steady lines keep their height, noise and random spikes shrink (about √N). Resolution does not improve.
g / mm/sAcceleration amplitude or velocity amplitude. Velocity emphasises low frequencies (roll orders), acceleration high ones (bearings, impacts).
Top peaksThree strongest lines per axis with frequency and amplitude.
Velocity RMSRMS of the velocity spectrum from 2 Hz (rolls up to 600 rpm or unknown speed) or 10 Hz (faster) to 123 Hz.
Band RMSAcceleration RMS in 2–10, 10–25, 25–50, 50–100 and 100–123 Hz. Shows where the energy is and where it moves.

Reading the chart

  • Red dashed lines mark 0.5x, 1x, 2x and 3x of the roll. A peak on a marker is caused by the roll rotation.
  • The markers come from the automatically found 1x; when no clear 1x is found there are no markers.
  • The rise at 0–1 Hz is window leakage of the removed gravity; ignore it.
  • Compare axes: the radial axes (perpendicular to the shaft) show unbalance; the axial axis shows misalignment more strongly.

Roll orders 0.5x / 1x / 2x / 3x

With the roll frequency known, the collector reports the velocity amplitude (mm/s) of the strongest line within ±3 % of each order.

How the roll speed is found

  • In every capture series the collector searches 60–1500 rpm (1–25 Hz) for the harmonic comb 1x + 2x + 3x in the velocity spectrum of all axes and refines it from the peak positions. Each roll is found on its own, whatever its diameter, and the result follows every speed change.
  • The result (rpm, 1x in Hz, confidence 0–1) is shown in the spectrum panel and stored in Grafana (Roll speed from vibration).
  • No clear comb (very smooth roll, strong foreign vibration, stopped line): no roll orders for that series.
  • If the 1x line is very weak and 2x strong, the 2x may be taken for 1x (rpm doubled). Check the rpm against the line once.
  • Sensors on neighbouring rolls can see each other's vibration; each sensor normally finds its own roll because that is strongest at its bearing.
  • Compare trends at similar speeds: unbalance grows roughly with the square of the speed.
  • 1x dominant and rising: unbalance or eccentricity.
  • 2x at or above 1x (ratio 2x/1x ≥ 0.5–1): misalignment, coupling problem, looseness.
  • 0.5x clearly present: rubbing or looseness.
  • Orders missing while the overall level stays: no clear roll comb was found in that series (see above).

How severe is it? (ISO 10816-3)

Velocity RMS (mm/s, broadband) of machines 15 kW … 50 MW measured on bearing housings. For 120–600 rpm the range starts at 2 Hz, above 600 rpm at 10 Hz.

MachineFoundationA/BB/CC/D
Group 2: 15–300 kWrigid1.42.84.5
Group 2: 15–300 kWflexible2.34.57.1
Group 1: 300 kW – 50 MWrigid2.34.57.1
Group 1: 300 kW – 50 MWflexible3.57.111.0

A newly commissioned · B unrestricted long-term operation · C limited operation, plan maintenance · D damage likely.

These limits are for whole machines on their bearings, not for every roll. Use them as orientation and rely on the trend against the machine's own baseline. Rolls in paper, film and print lines often have stricter process limits (marks, gauge variation).

Sensor harmonics (spectrum energy)

The sensor computes its own spectrum about once per second and reports energy at 8 focus points (0.25x … 6x), 6 bands and the total. The manual does not say which frequency is 1x and it cannot be set; field tests suggest a fixed reference of about 50 Hz unrelated to the roll. Energy units are not documented.

Treat these values as experimental. For roll diagnosis use the collector's own Spectrum (FFT) and roll orders; the sensor's total energy and band distribution are still useful as a trend.

Limits of the sensor

  • Specified range 5–100 Hz. A slow roll's 1x (e.g. 5.3 Hz) is near the lower edge; the sensor's own registers may filter it out, the FFT shows it.
  • Spectrum up to ≈ 123 Hz: rolling-bearing defect frequencies and their resonances are often higher, so kurtosis and crest factor are the main bearing indicators.
  • One capture is 4.16 s and 0.24 Hz resolution; it cannot be made longer. Averaging reduces noise only.
  • During a capture (≈ 4.5 s) polling of all sensors on the bus pauses; within a series every sensor is polled once between captures.
  • Mount the sensor rigidly on the bearing housing (magnet on a flat, clean surface or screw). A loose mount looks like looseness.

Simulation

For tests without a machine set VIBRATION_SIM_PORTS=1 in .env, rebuild the collector and select a sim:// port in the Bus form. sim://roll?speed=600&diameter=600 simulates a healthy directly driven roll; adding &scenario=faults runs a 30 minute sequence: normal, unbalance, misalignment, speed 450 m/min, looseness, bearing impacts, communication faults, normal. The current stage is shown in the Port tile. Rename the sensor for a session so simulated data stay apart from real data, and select the real port afterwards — the real sensor is not polled meanwhile.
